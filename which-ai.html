The standard job

Most real work is both. Always in this order.

1

Google gathers

Deep Research, or Notebook if it's your own files. Get the raw material and the citations.

2

Save it

Into Drive. Everyone skips this step, and it's why the work gets done twice.

3

Claude judges

Paste the findings in. What does it mean, what applies, what's missing, what's the risk.

4

Claude drafts

From the checked findings — never from memory. Memory is where inventions come from.

Warning signs

Gemini is drifting

STOP AND RESTART
  • "Great question! Let's unpack that"
  • Agreeing with something you know is wrong
  • Answering a question you didn't ask
  • Editing or creating files unprompted

Claude is drifting

STOP AND RESTART
  • Hedging on everything, committing to nothing
  • Confident claims with no source named
  • Longer answers that say less
  • Folding after you pushed back once

Both: when it drifts, start a new chat. Arguing it back on track costs more time than restarting.

Gemini Notebook — step by step

Create the workspace

Go to notebooklm.google.com. Click "+ New Notebook" and give it a strict project name. Pro gives you up to 500 notebooks — keep projects completely isolated.

Source ingestion

Open the left "Sources" panel and click "Add source". Upload from Drive (Docs, Sheets, PDFs, audio transcripts — up to 300 sources on Pro, max 500k words/200MB each). Or click "Web" to search the live internet directly inside the notebook.

Fast Research vs. Deep Research

Fast Research: when you need specific documents now. Scans the web in seconds and gives you a checklist of candidates. Review, check the relevant ones, click Import.

Deep Research: when starting empty. An agent browses dozens of sites for 2–5 minutes, drafts a comprehensive briefing, and automatically imports both the report and the cited sources into your notebook.

Query & verify

Select the checkboxes of the sources you want active. Type your prompt. Every fact includes a numbered citation tag — click it to jump to the exact source text. Zero guessing, zero hallucinating beyond your source stack.

Deep Research — running one properly

Path A: In-Notebook Research

Where: Sources Panel → Web → Select "Deep Research".

Function: Ingests findings directly into your notebook.

Pacing: Runs 2–5 minutes in background.

Best for: Populating a brand-new project with a synthesized briefing report and curated external references without context switching.

Path B: Macro Research in Gemini App

Where: gemini.google.com — click the tools (+) button in the prompt bar → select the "Deep Research" chip.

Function: Generates an exhaustive standalone report (5–15+ pages).

Pacing: 5 to 15 minutes. Gemini first generates an editable research plan.

Review the plan: Always review the plan card before starting. Delete irrelevant search branches; add mandatory regulatory or technical angles. Click "Start Research".

Saving: Export directly to Google Docs (one click), or copy into Gemini Notebook for source-grounded cross-examination.

When to use what

Standard Notebook ingestion

You already have proprietary PDFs, contracts, spreadsheets, or internal transcripts that the public internet cannot see.

Fast Research

You know the topic, you just need 3–5 current articles, official guidelines, or press releases added to your notebook right now.

Deep Research

You are mapping unfamiliar territory, conducting multi-vector market research, or synthesizing cross-disciplinary policy without existing documentation.

Critical limitations

Know these before you start

  • Frozen snapshots: Web sources pulled via Fast or Deep Research are static snapshots taken at import time. If the live website changes, the notebook will not know unless you manually delete and re-import.
  • Source dilution risk: Deep Research can pull tertiary summaries, forum threads, or SEO blog posts. Inspect the discovered source list before leaving them active.
  • Hard silos: Notebooks cannot read other notebooks. Pro's 300-source limit applies inside a single notebook boundary.
