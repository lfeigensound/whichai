Models in detail

Four names in the picker. You'll use two.

Sonnet by default.
Opus when it matters.

If you never read past this line, you'll be fine.

The four

Sonnet 5 — start here

Use for: almost everything. Reading documents, analysis, drafting, questions, summarising.

Why: fast, and the huge memory means you can paste a whole regulation in without chopping it up.

Don't: reach for Opus first out of habit. Sonnet handles most of what you do, and burns less of your weekly allowance.

Opus 5 — the hard ones

Use for: cross-referencing several regulations at once. Spotting what's missing. Anything where being confidently wrong would cost you in front of a client or board.

Why: better at holding many constraints at once without losing one halfway through.

Cost: uses your allowance faster. Worth it on the hard 10%, wasteful on the routine 90%.

Haiku 4.5 — quick and small

Use for: reformatting, tidying a list, short factual lookups, turning notes into bullets.

Don't: give it anything needing judgement, or a very long document — its memory is much smaller.

Fable 5.1 — not for you

Built for heavy coding work. On Pro it's metered separately rather than included. Ignore it.

Extended thinking

The toggle worth knowing

Makes the model work through a problem before answering instead of replying straight away. Slower, and uses more of your allowance.

Turn it on for: "does this apply to us", multi-step reasoning, anything where you want the working shown.

Leave it off for: drafting, summarising, reformatting, quick questions.

How the allowance works

Two limits at once

A rolling 5-hour session limit and a weekly cap. Anthropic doesn't publish exact token numbers for either, so nobody can tell you "you get X messages".

What this means in practice: heavy Opus use in one afternoon can slow you down that evening. Using Sonnet for routine work keeps Opus available for when you need it.

If you hit a limit

  • It resets — the app tells you when
  • Switch to a smaller model rather than waiting
  • Repeated limits mid-week means you're using Opus for routine work

Projects

Make one. Never re-explain yourself again.

A Project holds your standing context — who you are, your organisation, your terminology, how you want answers formatted. It loads into every chat inside that Project automatically.

As you add more documents, Claude switches to a retrieval mode to handle the volume. You don't need to manage that.

This is the fix for "it forgets half my prompt."

To fill in: your standing brief

Paste the exact text you put into your Project, so you can rebuild it if you ever lose it.
